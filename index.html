Expert-Led FAQ
Common questions, answered clearly
What is your approach to treating complex anxiety and OCD?

We rely on rigorous, evidence-based frameworks, primarily Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT). Drawing on Dr. Chakraborty's published research on adapting CBT for OCD and managing anxiety, we tailor these gold-standard treatments to your unique neurobiological and psychosocial context.

Do you provide trauma-informed care?

Yes. Trauma profoundly impacts both the mind and the body. Our approach integrates cognitive skills training and emotional stabilization — areas our team has extensively researched and published on. We ensure therapy proceeds at a safe pace, focusing on building emotional regulation before processing traumatic memories.

How effective is therapy for severe emotional and personality-related challenges?

Highly effective when paired with the right clinical modality. Dr. Das specializes in Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), a treatment specifically designed for emotional dysregulation and personality vulnerabilities. Through consistent skills training in distress tolerance and mindfulness, patients see significant, long-term improvements in their daily functioning.

Do you address women's mental health and life-cycle transitions?

Women's mental health requires a nuanced understanding of biological and relational factors. Drawing from our clinical research on Interpersonal Therapy (IPT) in female infertility and life-cycle transitions, we provide highly specialized care for perinatal mood changes, infertility-related stress, and gender-based trauma.

What does neurodiversity mean?

Neurodiversity is a general term for the different ways people think, learn, communicate, and process information. Therapy can help a person understand their needs, build on their strengths, and find practical ways to manage everyday challenges.

How can therapy support an autistic person?

Therapy can offer a calm and respectful space to work on stress, communication, routines, relationships, or sensory needs. The aim is not to change who the person is, but to support comfort, confidence, and daily wellbeing.

Can therapy help with learning disabilities?

Therapy does not remove a learning disability, but it can help with frustration, low confidence, anxiety, and difficulties at school or work. Support may also include simple coping strategies and guidance for families.

When should someone seek help for depression?

It may be helpful to seek support when sadness, low energy, loss of interest, sleep changes, or hopelessness continue and start affecting daily life. Therapy can help a person understand what they are going through and develop healthier ways to cope.

How can couples therapy help a relationship?

Couples therapy gives both partners a safe place to talk about concerns, understand repeated patterns, and improve communication. The therapist does not take sides and instead helps the couple work towards practical and respectful changes.
